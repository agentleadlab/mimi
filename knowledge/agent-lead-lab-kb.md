## 2. GLOSSARY — INTERNAL TERMS

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **CPL** | Cost per lead. Spend ÷ leads. |
| **CAC** | Cost per sale. Spend ÷ **closes**. The real efficiency metric. |
| **TS-CAC** | Spend ÷ **total sales**. Looser than CAC — see below. |
| **Closes** | Sales with AP recorded in the command center. |
| **Total Sales** | Closes **plus** sales an agent marked sold without entering AP. Always ≥ closes. |
| **AP** | Annual premium. The revenue number. |
| **Avg AP** | AP ÷ closes. Typically $1,300–$3,700 depending on vertical. |
| **ROAS** | AP ÷ spend. |
| **DTC** | Days to close. Ranges 0.1–32 days on this account. |
| **Freq** | Frequency — average times a person saw the ad. |
| **ABO** | Ad-set budget optimization. Budget set per ad set. |
| **CBO** | Campaign budget optimization. Meta distributes budget across ad sets. |
| **TALP** | Base / deployment targeting (Fort Benning, Camp Pendleton, Okinawa, Vietnam, etc.) |
| **Vet 2.0 / Call in option** | A creative family, launched 9/13–9/17. |
| **Top Sales — [State]** | State-targeted ad sets. |
| **Stack** | Audience stacking tests (Spouse Stack, CDL Stack). |
| **"Lag"** | Dashboard flag: closes are real but the leads were bought before this date range. |

**Important:** "Closes" and "Total Sales" are different columns in the command center and they diverge. As of Oct 16: **1,387 closes vs 1,502 total sales** across VET + Truckers — an 8% gap *inside the dashboard*, before counting agents who post nothing at all.

## 8. CREATIVE LEARNINGS — the durable part

### 8.1 Cheap leads are not cheap sales
This is the most important pattern on the account and it has held across every window measured.

CPL bands, 7-day Oct 3 read:
| CPL band | Spend | CAC | ROAS |
|---|---|---|---|
| Under $25 | $4,800 | **$960** | 1.6x |
| **$25–30** | $15,771 | **$451** | 3.5x |
| **$30–35** | $10,884 | **$495** | 4.5x |
| $35–45 | $16,506 | $917 | 2.2x |
| $45+ | $7,045 | **$7,045** | 0.2x |

**The sweet spot is $25–$35 CPL.** Under $25 is a trap — half the lead price, roughly twice the cost per sale. Over $45 is dead weight.

When steering on CPL, use a **floor as well as a ceiling**.

### 8.2 Diagnosing a high CPL — traffic problem or form problem
**Click-to-lead rate = CPC ÷ CPL.** Account median is **6.4%**.

- High CPC (>$2.40), normal click-to-lead → overpaying for traffic. Fix audience/placement/bid.
- Normal CPC, low click-to-lead (<4.5%) → form or offer is losing people who already arrived. **Fix the landing page.**
- Both → structurally broken. Rebuild or close.

Worked example: **Vet 2.0 9/13 V.3** had a $1.17 CPC — the cheapest traffic on the account — and a 3.3% click-to-lead rate, half the median. Buying attention well, losing it at the form. That was a landing page fix worth $4,439 a week, invisible in the CPL column.

### 8.3 CTR does not predict sales
| CTR band | CAC |
|---|---|
| Under 2.5% | $780 |
| 2.5–3.0% | $830 |
| **3.0–4.0%** | **$511** |
| 4.0%+ | **$992** |

The highest-CTR bucket has the worst CAC. Vet 2.0 V.6 sits in it at a 5.38% CTR and a $933–$2,356 CAC depending on window. **CTR measures interest, not intent. Never optimize on it here.**

### 8.4 Hook structure — what the audience actually responds to
Discovered Oct 7 from a Veterans Facebook group post.

The posts veterans engage with organically are **not** "Virginia veterans." They are **"Virginia veterans rated 100%."** Three layers of specificity:
1. **State**
2. **Disability rating**
3. **Something earned and unclaimed**

Current state callouts stop at layer 1. The account runs state callouts (19 ad sets, $39,597) and disability-rating angles (3 ad sets, $3,287) **separately — never crossed.**

Early signal: the **80% Disability Rating** ad set pulled a **9.2% click-to-lead rate against a 6.4% median** on $139 of spend. Tiny sample, right direction.

**The emotional engine is loss aversion on something already earned** — not "here's an offer" but "you've already got this and you're leaving it on the table."

### 8.5 The current state callout script
> "If you're a [State] veteran you can qualify for a program most veterans have not heard about. You can qualify for up to $50,000 in permanent life insurance plans with day one coverage. No medical exams, no rate increases. Just guaranteed protection. VA programs make you wait 2 years. Ours protect you immediately. If you want to see exactly what you qualify for click learn more."

**The lead is buried in sentence five.** "VA programs make you wait 2 years. Ours protect you immediately" is the entire differentiator and it sits behind a generic curiosity opener.

**Accuracy note:** the 2-year wait is **VALife** specifically — the program for veterans with service-connected disabilities that replaced S-DVI in 2023. **VGLI does not work that way** (no health review within 240 days of separation, no 2-year wait). Saying "VA programs" as a category overstates it. Naming VALife is both more accurate and sharper. VALife also caps at $40,000 against our $50,000 — a second real comparison, currently unused.

### 8.6 Approved hook directions
**Angle — the thing nobody says (strongest)**
- "Your VA disability check stops the day you do. Your mortgage doesn't."
- "You're rated 80%. Your family isn't."
- "[State] veterans rated 100%: VALife makes you wait two years for coverage. Ours starts day one."

**Angle — the assumption**
- "Most disabled veterans assume no one will cover them. Most are wrong."
- "Rated 70% or higher in [State]? You've probably been told no before. That's not the whole story."

**Angle — timing / the window**
- "SGLI ended 120 days after you separated. Most veterans find out years later."

**Angle — confession / first person**
- "22 years in. Rated 90%. I still couldn't answer my wife's question."

**Angle — narrower than the state**
- "Veterans near [Fort Benning / Robins AFB]..." — extends TALP, which pulls a $17.77 CPL in ABO.

### 8.7 Copy rules for this account
- Front-load the callout. "[State] veterans rated at 100%" beats "If you're a [State] veteran..." — the qualification lands in word one instead of word four.
- Keep hooks **under 125 characters** so they survive Facebook's mobile truncation.
- Avoid **"veteran protection program"** and similar euphemisms. Vague + "program" reads as governmental, and vagueness is what makes the category feel scammy.
- Avoid **"you qualify"** — the ad cannot make an underwriting determination.
- Avoid **"guaranteed"** — restricted language in insurance advertising in several states.
- Replace invented curiosity with **a true, specific, uncomfortable fact.** Verifiable beats mysterious.
- Specific numbers beat ranges. "$47,300" not "a lot of money."

### 8.8 Compliance line — do not cross
We sell life insurance. We are **not** the VA, a VSO, or a benefits service.

Never imply affiliation with a government program, never frame an ad as a benefits checklist or unclaimed entitlements, never promise benefits we don't provide. A veteran clicking expecting help with a property tax exemption and landing on an insurance form is a Meta policy problem, a regulator problem, and a real-harm problem — in that order of how fast it costs us.

**Use the structure of what works organically. Never the subject.**

Any claim about VA programs, waiting periods, coverage amounts, or enrollment figures needs verification and compliance sign-off before it runs.

### 8.9 Audience overlap / self-competition
Meta runs each campaign as a **separate bidder**. Two of our campaigns wanting the same person bid against each other and raise our own price.

"All USA" already includes Georgia. A national campaign plus a "national + best states" campaign is **two national campaigns with 100% overlap.**

**A real split requires geographic exclusion:** Campaign A = national *excluding* the target states; Campaign B = those states only. Form-based exclusions only remove people who already submitted a form — a few hundred out of a 1.3M-person reach — and do nothing about cold traffic overlap.

**Note for forecasting:** adding form exclusions will push measured **CPL up**, because we stop counting cheap repeat leads. That is the correct trade and should be flagged in advance so it doesn't read as a failure.

## 9. METRIC HIERARCHY — how to judge an ad set

**Rank by CAC. Use CPL as a sanity check, never as the decision.**

1. **CAC** (spend ÷ closes) — the decision metric. Needs ~30 days for attribution to settle.
2. **ROAS** — catches high-AP ad sets that look mediocre on CAC.
3. **Click-to-lead rate** — the best *early* signal available with no sales data at all. Pure Meta data.
4. **CPL** — only inside the $25–$35 band. Outside it, both directions are warnings.
5. **CTR** — background information. Does not predict sales.
6. **Frequency** — see section 11.3. No longer a cut or rotate trigger.

### Minimum volume before judging
- Under 12 leads: the CPL is noise from a tiny denominator, not a creative verdict.
- Under ~50 leads: a zero-close result means nothing. At a ~6% close rate, a 20-lead ad set shows zero closes 29% of the time by chance alone.
- **No cut recommendations on 7-day data. Flag for review instead.**

## 11. CORRECTIONS LOG — things we believed that turned out wrong

**Read this before asserting any pattern confidently.** The recurring cause is almost always the same: short windows plus attribution lag.

### 11.1 Attribution lag is the dominant analytical problem
Roughly **30% of closes land on ad sets that spent nothing in a 7-day window.** Days-to-close runs 0.1 to 32 days. Every 7-day read on this account understates closes and produces false kill signals.

**Consequence:** 30-day windows for decisions. 7-day windows for monitoring only.

### 11.2 Marginal CAC — the Oct 2 call was wrong
Oct 2 measured marginal CAC at **$2,697 — 9.9x blended** — and recommended staging the spend increase rather than taking it in one step.

Oct 16 showed the account absorbed a **2.3x increase at a $420 marginal CAC against a $409 blend — 1.03x.** No scaling penalty at all. The Oct 2 figure was distorted by lag on a window that had barely matured.

### 11.3 Frequency is NOT a leading indicator
**Reported Oct 3 (7-day):** frequency ≤1.3 → 5.69% close rate, $560 CAC. Frequency 1.4–1.9 → 2.40%, ~$1,400. Recommendation was to rotate creative before 1.4.

**Oct 16 (30-day):**
| Freq | CAC | Close rate |
|---|---|---|
| ≤1.3 | $424 | 6.74% |
| 1.4–1.6 | $497 | 5.66% |
| **1.7–1.9** | **$332** | **8.04%** |
| 2.0+ | $403 | 7.43% |

No monotonic relationship. The 1.7–1.9 band has the **best** CAC and close rate.

The 7-day pattern was attribution lag: new ad sets have low frequency *and* few recorded closes, and the two moved together for a non-causal reason.

**Stop using frequency as a cut or rotate trigger.**

### 11.4 Other reversals
- **"OTP beats AI 5:1"** → corrected to 1.8x once full CBO data arrived. Original figure came from a single-ad sample.
- **"5x LP-vs-Forms CAC gap"** → vanished at 30 days ($581 vs $570, a 2% difference).
- **"Momentum IUL is broken"** (376 leads, 0 closes) → attribution lag. Resolved to 23 closes at 30 days.
- **Seven+ wrong cut recommendations** across three weeks: Ohio, Louisiana, Black Box, US TEST 2, both OPEN ENDED, TRE New Angles 3, California. All recovered after being recommended for cutting.
- **CBO consolidation reversed twice** — "consolidate into Top States," then "Entire US is winning," then Total Sales data showed Top States actually won ($195 vs $248). Root cause: differential AP-recording rates between the two.
- **53% CAC understatement across all reports before Sep 17**, discovered when the Total Sales column appeared (1,270 total sales vs 833 closes).

### 11.5 The standing rule this produced
> No cut recommendations on 7-day data. Flag for review instead. A pattern is not real until it holds on 30 days.

## 12. HOUSE STYLE

### Voice
- Write like a human. Avoid AI-sounding copy.
- Personal-casual, clear, witty but not cringey. Empathetic yet direct.
- No jargon, fluff, or guru hype.
- No poetic or metaphorical language.
- Short, clear, high-converting copy for ads and social.
- End outputs with actionable next steps or options.

### Ad copy parameters
- Target: **ages 40–85, USA**, Facebook.
- Verticals: Mortgage Protection, Final Expense, IUL, Veterans, Trucker/CDL.
- Short. Punchy. Then a longer sentence that lets the reader feel it. Then short again.
- Write to one person, not a crowd.
- Specificity over generality, always.

### Avoid
- Corporate AI-speak ("In today's competitive landscape…")
- Hedge words ("might," "could potentially," "may help")
- Feature dumps without emotional payoff
- Generic openers ("Are you a veteran struggling with…")
- Anything that reads like every other ad in the feed

### Deliverable formats
- Reports: Google Doc + Discord block + PPTX deck
- Discord: **never use tables**
- Decks: dark theme (#0B0F0E background, #22C55E green accent), drawn bar charts not native PowerPoint charts (native charts drop category labels), CPL and CAC on every data view
